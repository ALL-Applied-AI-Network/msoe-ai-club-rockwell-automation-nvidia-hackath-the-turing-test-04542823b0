## 2. Where It Falls Short

We report our limitations, assumptions, and failure boundaries honestly:

1. **One recorded run:** All physical camera evaluations are based on a single continuous recording (Video 5). The system has not yet been evaluated across different operating days, shifts, or long-term hardware wear.
2. **One human judge:** Our visual ground truth comes from a single human evaluator (`judge_1`). On borderline vials (vials 7, 10, 16, 29), the judge noted "unsure", meaning "8 of 8" reflects alignment with this human scorer's criteria when "unsure" is counted as off, rather than an objective universal truth.
3. **Only 30 vials total:** The dataset contains only 30 physical vials across 10 recipes, with each recipe appearing as a single 3-vial batch.
4. **Few small vials:** Only 4 scored vials in Video 5 are small 30–40 mL fills (vials 1, 2, 4, 5). Because small fills do not reach the high-contrast HIGH patch in front of the white backdrop, small-vial results are reported strictly as counts and cannot support statistical claims.
5. **Light from one side fools the check:** Blank normalization cancels only uniform, even changes in illumination. In the Card 7 digital twin test, strongly asymmetric light from one side falsely flagged all 4 clean vials tested.
6. **Cosmos explanations need human review:** Cosmos achieved only 1 of 8 fully correct explanations on first repeats, despite claiming high confidence on all 24 of 24 calls. It hallucinated hardware defects ("misrouted tubing", incorrect dispense sequences) and nonexistent chemicals ("Recipe 12 cleaning solution"). AI explanations cannot be presented directly to operators without human review and approval.
7. **The twin-trained model lost to simple color math:** Despite calibrating the Isaac Sim digital twin's fill height to within 3.5 px and applying camera response gains, Random Forest regressors trained on twin images achieved only 5 of 8 (or 4 of 8) catches on tall vials, compared to 8 of 8 for simple CIELAB color math. Synthetic appearance variation helped test robustness, but did not beat simple physical color measurement.
